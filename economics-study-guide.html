Economics Assignment – Study Guide

Demand, supply, elasticity, percentage changes and government price control

1. Types of price elasticity of demand

Unitary elastic (Ed = 1)

Percentage change in quantity demanded equals percentage change in price.

Fairly inelastic (Ed less than 1)

Quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than price.

Fairly elastic (Ed greater than 1)

Quantity demanded changes by a greater percentage than price.

Perfectly inelastic (Ed = 0)

Quantity demanded does not change when price changes.

For every graph: vertical axis = Price (P); horizontal axis = Quantity demanded (Q); D = demand curve.

2. Four factors affecting elasticity of demand

  1. Availability of substitutes: More substitutes usually make demand more elastic.
  2. Nature of the good: Necessities tend to be inelastic; luxuries tend to be elastic.
  3. Consumers' income: Income affects how strongly buyers respond to price changes.
  4. Proportion of income spent: Expensive items taking a large share of income tend to have more elastic demand.

3. Changes in demand and supply

D shifts right = demand increases; D shifts left = demand decreases. S shifts right = supply increases; S shifts left = supply decreases.

(a) Demand decreases; supply increases

Equilibrium price falls. Quantity is uncertain.

(b) Demand increases; supply decreases

Equilibrium price rises. Quantity is uncertain.

(c) Demand and supply both increase

Equilibrium quantity rises. Price is uncertain.

4. Percentage change calculations

Formula:
Percentage change = (New value − Old value) ÷ Old value × 100

Use the first figure as the old value and the second as the new value.

5. Government price control

(a) Minimum price (price floor)

The lowest legal price. When set above equilibrium, it can protect producers but cause a surplus (Qs > Qd).

(b) Maximum price (price ceiling)

The highest legal price. When set below equilibrium, it can help consumers but cause a shortage (Qd > Qs).

(c) Price control under demand and supply

Price control is government regulation of market prices. It includes minimum price (price floor) and maximum price (price ceiling), used to protect producers or consumers.

Quick revision

  • Demand curve slopes downwards; supply curve slopes upwards.
  • Demand right = increase; demand left = decrease.
  • Supply right = increase; supply left = decrease.
  • Price floor above equilibrium may cause a surplus.
  • Price ceiling below equilibrium may cause a shortage.
